Spotting a leak and deciding what to do

A leak repair visit can help address an active drip, a damp patch, or water appearing where it shouldn’t. The first step is to reduce the chance of further damage: move items away from the wet area, catch dripping water if you can, and shut off the water supply if you know how to do so safely. If water is near electrical equipment, stay clear and seek appropriate help.

Before calling, note where you first saw water, when it appeared, and whether it changes when a faucet or appliance is used. These details can help explain the situation. Don’t assume the visible wet spot marks the leak itself. Water may travel before it shows, so avoid opening walls or floors to search for a hidden source.

What leak repair may involve

The plumber will need to understand where water is showing and what fixtures or plumbing are nearby. Depending on what can be seen and the source of the problem, leak repair may involve checking accessible connections, a fixture, or a section of pipe. The next steps depend on what the plumber finds, so ask for an explanation of the suspected source and the proposed work before repairs begin.

You can prepare by clearing a path to the wet area and nearby plumbing, if it’s safe. Keep children and pets away from standing water and tools. Avoid applying sealants or taking apart a fitting as a temporary fix, since that can make the source harder to assess or worsen the leak. If you’ve already tried something, tell the plumber what you did.

Questions people ask about Leak Repair Louisville

What should I do before a plumber arrives for a leak?

Move belongings away from the water and catch drips if it’s safe. If you know how to shut off the water safely, do so, and mention any steps you took when you call.

Can I tell where a leak is coming from by looking at the wet spot?

Not always, because water can travel before it becomes visible. Describe where you first noticed it and let the plumber assess the likely source.

When should I call about a leak?

Call if water is actively flowing, spreading, or showing up where you can’t identify the cause. If it’s near electrical equipment, keep clear and seek appropriate help.
